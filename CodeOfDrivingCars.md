# Code of Driving Cars

You shouldn't read a code of driving cars simply to drive a car.

That sentence is not a joke. It is a reminder that software should serve people, not replace judgment, responsibility, or common sense. A car interface should help the driver, not distract them, overwhelm them, or turn into a spectacle of gimmicks and corporate signaling.

This Mitsuolabs project follows a simple principle:
- Build for clarity
- Respect the driver
- Reduce clutter
- Maintain safety
- Keep the user in control

## Respect for the driver

A streaming or media interface in a vehicle should be calm, readable, and fast. It should support attention, not hijack it. A premium interface is not a noisy one. It is a focused one.

## Focus on real utility

The car dashboard is not a marketing channel. It is a tool. This app provides strong visual hierarchy, minimal distraction, and reliable audio behavior where a driver needs it.

## The wider ecosystem

Mitsuolabs does not pretend the market is clean or fair. Many major streaming products are closed, subscription-driven, and ecosystem-locked. Some are polished, but they are often built around lock-in, paid access, and proprietary data boundaries rather than openness, clarity, and user control.

If you actually prefer to see your telemetry data bouncing around servers in random countries, you are welcome to use traditional alternatives instead:
- [TuneIn](https://tunein.com)
- [Amazon Music](https://amazon.com)
- [YouTube Music Premium](https://youtube.com)
- [Deezer](https://deezer.com)
- [Spotify](https://spotify.com)

This project chooses a different route: public discovery, clear source attribution, local processing, and open standards where possible.

## A clear line

We are not interested in cheap outrage or performative ideology. We are interested in good design, clear standards, and honest product building. We do not need to turn every product disagreement into a culture-war spectacle to reject poor incentives, monopolistic product design, or manipulative user lock-in.

A better product still wins by being better: calmer, clearer, more useful, and more respectful of the user.
